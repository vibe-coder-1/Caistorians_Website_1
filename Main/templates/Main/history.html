History of CGS

1630

School founded using money from the wills of Francis Rawlinson and William Hansard

1631

The school is constructed in what is now known as the Old Hall

1634

The first headmaster, Mr Metcalf is appointed

1700 to 1900

Grove House is built and used for teaching. It was demolished in the 1980's due to subsidence

1818

By this point 30 to 40 pupils attended the school

1837

The school building is repaired with a parapet being built on the South side

1838

W. Ellis LLD, the headmaster, announces the reception for boarding pupils is complete

1885

The patrons, the Ayscoughs, surrender their position to a board of governers

1904

Lindsey County Council did not recognise the school as efficient so pupils had to go to Market Rasen's De Aston school for grammar education. This had led to only 7 pupils being taught at CGS by this point.

1908

After adopting suggestions from the council such as allowing girls to study there, the school became considered efficient, leading to a rise of pupils.

1909

2 extra rooms were built for the library, before being converted into one science lab

1910

There were now 46 pupils studying at CGS in this year

1914

A new science lab is built, which has since been converted into a kitchen, language lab, and is now our RE room. Also, the girls toilets in the old building are built and the playing field on Navigation lane is bought

1920

A large army hut is aquired to be used for 3 new classrooms

1921

Another boarding house is aquired to further increase pupil numbers

1930

The new main building opened including more classrooms

1931

Major repairs are made to the Old Hall

20th and 21st Century

From this point, many temporary buildings have been used to teach in during periods of repairs and building works on the school site

1944

Before this, the local education authority paid fees for all pupils who passed a scholarship test, whilst parents paid for those who did not. 1944 marked the point when an 11+ test was introduced with those not passing not being admitted to the school, marking the end of fee paying pupils

1945

Pre fabricated buildings are used to expand the school

1950's

The chapel is used at the top of the school for teaching

1957

Council announces plans to close the school as girls are beginning to be admitted to De Aston, so they reasoned the school was no longer required

1960

A walk took place in protest of the school closing on the night of the 18th February

1961

A music room is built in a temporary building which later became a history room before being demolished

1964

The closure plan for the school is abandoned, and a new boarding house is planned to be built (Lindsey house)

1965

The needlework room is built which is later converted to a geography room before being demolished. Around the same time, beech house is aquired and a swimming pool is built

1967

Lindsey house is complete

1981

By this point, 327 pupils attend CGS

1983

The chapel is converted to a library

1984

Manning building opens and there is an extension to the science block

1988

With the new guidence allowing parents to choose which school their child went to, the school introduced its own entrance test

1993-1994

The technology and science buildings open across these 2 years

1995

The school closes to boarding pupils and so Lindsey is converted into a music classroom and a flat for French and German exchange students

1996-2016

Roger Hale is headmaster and by 2016 there are 560 pupils in the school. Many construction works occur in these 20 years

2001

Lindsey is converted for 2 maths classrooms and 4 English classrooms on the top 2 floors of Lindsey house

2005

Manning is extended to include 2 new classrooms, a fitness suite and a computer classroom for PE students, with the school being awarded sports college status

2010

The school is awarded acadamy status

2011

Food technology being planned to become a compulsary subject leads to an extension on the canteen, with the music department being extended also

2012

Olympic Torch building is built to commemerate students involved in the 2012 Olympic games. This building has 2 classrooms for the physics department

2022

Grove court is built

2023

The new hall is rebuilt and called the Newbolt centre

2026

Currently, there are 650 pupils at CGS, which has recently been awarded the 'Best State School in the East Midlands' by the Sunday Times
